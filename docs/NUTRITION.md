# Executive Nutrition

## Scope and consent
This is a practical meal-planning calculator for healthy adult men, not a clinical diet. The UI must require confirmation that the user tolerates the displayed foods before applying the plan. It includes dairy, eggs, wheat, oats and nuts; allergies require ingredient-level review. There is no fish, seafood, onion, salad leaves or rejected vegetable in the default menu. Accepted plants include tomato, cucumber, avocado, potato, sweet potato and fruit. Tomato sauce means plain crushed tomato, not a mixed vegetable sauce. Legumes are not included until tolerance is confirmed.

## Estimation
`targets(profile)` uses Mifflin–St Jeor's male equation: 10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × years + 5. Activity multipliers are 1.35 / 1.55 / 1.75; these are heuristic starting estimates, not measured expenditure. Goal `recomp` or `fat` uses a 10% deficit, `muscle` a 5% surplus, `maintenance` no adjustment. Reference protein is 1.8 g/kg/day; fat reference 0.9 g/kg/day; carbohydrate fills the remaining calculated energy. A missing/invalid profile produces no personalized target. Values outside age 18–85, height 130–220 cm, weight 40–220 kg or BMI 18.5–40 are not automatically calculated. A true `medical` flag also disables calculation. These limits do not establish clinical safety inside them; illnesses, medication and additional restrictions need individualized professional review.

Scale actual protein-source and starch/fat-source quantities separately to approximate both protein and energy targets. Keep fruit and accepted vegetables present. The bounded scaling factors prevent implausibly extreme portions. Display both the target and actual calculated menu totals; they may differ. Daily totals and every meal's macros are calculated from the exact displayed gram amounts. User overrides are authoritative and are not secretly rescaled; substitutions therefore can change totals. Targets are estimates to revise using 2–4 weeks of trends, appetite, performance and recovery, never a promise of fat loss. No spot reduction is claimed.

## Weights and food data
Amounts are edible weights. Meat, potatoes and eggs: raw; rice, oats and pasta: dry; dairy and bread: as sold; fruit: edible portion; oil: grams. Milk grams approximately equal millilitres. Egg weights can be translated at about 50 g per shelled egg. Raw and cooked weights cannot be interchanged. Food values are representative, rounded planning assumptions stored directly in `foods`, not a live certified database or brand-specific laboratory values. Calories use 4 kcal/g protein, 4 kcal/g available carbohydrate, 9 kcal/g fat; no extra fiber energy is added. Product labels and cooking variation will differ. Kitchen oil must be counted rather than silently omitted.

## Seven-day menu and substitutions
Four main meals per day follow the requested weekly base menu. Pretraining banana is an optional separate snack and is not included in default totals or shopping list. If consumed, count it or move a fruit portion from the existing menu. Meal alternatives rotate within the same slot across the seven-day menu, making them practical substitutes without claiming identical macros. Shopping list aggregates exactly the current seven-day plan, including overrides. An `overrides` key `dayIndex:mealIndex` supplies a full `{name,time,items}` meal with quantities already adjusted. Favorites and schedules can be stored by the UI without remote services.

## Nutrition quality
Whole grains, oats, fruit, avocado and nuts improve fiber variety but do not guarantee adequacy. Display actual estimated fiber and discuss approximately 25–30 g/day as a practical general reference; increase gradually with fluids. The database does not calculate micronutrients, so do not claim calcium, potassium, iron or vitamin sufficiency. Dairy can help calcium intake, potatoes/fruit can contribute potassium, fruit vitamin C, and eggs and meat different micronutrients. Restricted food variety can leave gaps despite sufficient protein. Do not automatically prescribe supplements.

## Afternoon training / preparation
If several hours have elapsed since lunch, a tolerated light snack 60–120 minutes before exercise can help comfort and energy. Water before/during training and a normal protein-containing dinner afterward are practical; no narrow anabolic deadline is required. Avoid using stimulants as the default fatigue solution. Protect sleep. Batch-cook rice/potatoes and protein, refrigerate promptly and freeze later portions; do not recommend keeping all seven days in the fridge. Quick choices: plain high-protein yogurt plus fruit, cooked plain chicken with microwave rice, or eggs plus whole-grain bread, subject to labels and tolerance.
